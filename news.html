Red Thread unspools a quiet, luminous hour

Mira Vale's new record trades bombast for patience — and finds something enormous in the hush.

By the Listening Desk4 min 18 sec

The record's centrepiece drifts between tape hiss and cathedral reverb.

There is a moment, roughly two minutes in, when the low end simply stops — and the room keeps going. It is the kind of trick that reads as an accident until it happens a second time, and then a third, and you understand it has been engineered all along.

Vale has always written in negative space, but here the negative space is the song. The arrangement resists the urge to fill: a single sustained tone, a brushed snare, a reverb tail that outlives its source. What could be empty instead feels attentive.

The album's arc is deliberately unhurried. Tracks bleed into one another without a seam, and the sequencing rewards a full listen over a shuffled one. It is music that assumes you have nowhere else to be.

By the closing minutes the record has circled back to its opening figure, altered by everything in between. It ends, appropriately, mid-breath — as if the next note were simply waiting for you to press play again.
